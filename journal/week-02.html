London and Chengdu: Different Seasonal Climate Patterns

The question

How does the seasonal climate pattern at my London site compare with Chengdu, and how does this comparison relate to the global distribution and density of climate stations?

The data

I used Esri's World Historical Climate — Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981–2010, based on NOAA NCEI's Global Historical Climatology Network-Daily (GHCN-Daily) v3.24. The dataset contains monthly average temperature and precipitation from climate stations with sufficiently complete records between 1981 and 2010. I accessed the dataset in September 2026 and used the 1981–2010 reference period for both London and Chengdu.

The method

I first viewed the full global station layer to examine the distribution and density of climate stations. I then identified Heathrow (GHCN-D ID: UKM00003772) as the station representing my London site and Chengdu (GHCN-D ID: CHM00056294) as the comparison station. I created bar charts using the twelve monthly average temperature fields and the twelve monthly average precipitation fields. I used mean aggregation to compare the monthly values for the two stations.

The evidence

What I found

The global station map appears to show relatively dense coverage in many low-latitude areas, including regions around the equator, although station density varies substantially between regions. The London and Chengdu comparison shows a clear difference in seasonal climate. London has a relatively moderate seasonal temperature range and precipitation distributed throughout the year. Chengdu has warmer summers and a much stronger summer precipitation peak, with considerably drier winter months.

These differences can be partly explained by geographic location. Chengdu is at a lower latitude and has a subtropical, monsoon-influenced climate, while London is much farther north and has a maritime climate. Topography also matters: Chengdu is located in the Sichuan Basin, with the Tibetan Plateau and surrounding mountain systems to the west. London, in contrast, is strongly influenced by its surrounding ocean. These geographic and topographic differences help explain the different seasonal patterns of temperature and precipitation shown in the charts.

What this does not show

This comparison uses the 1981–2010 reference period, which is older than the current 1991–2020 climate normal. The Heathrow station represents one location near London rather than the entire city, and the Chengdu station similarly represents one measurement site. The uneven distribution of stations also means that areas with fewer points should not be interpreted as having less climate variability. Finally, the map projection can affect how spatial density appears visually.
