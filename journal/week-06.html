Land Cover, Then and Now

The question

How did land cover in central London change between 2017 and 2024, and what kinds of urban change remain invisible to land-cover maps? At 265 Strand, a commercial building was converted into NYU London's academic centre. This change benefited students and staff by providing university facilities. It also raises questions about how patterns and distributions of land-cover classes represent physical change while leaving changes in building use less visible.

The data

I used the Sentinel-2 10 m Land Use/Land Cover Time Series produced by Esri and Impact Observatory. I selected 2017 and 2024 for the main comparison because NYU London moved into its new academic centre at 265 Strand in autumn 2024. This made 2024 a meaningful date for examining land-cover patterns around the time of the site's change in use. I also compared 2017 with 2025 as a third-date check. The dataset has a native spatial resolution of 10 m per pixel. The layer is provided under the Esri Master License Agreement, while the source land-cover data is licensed under CC BY 4.0.

The method

I used the Land Cover Explorer in LAND COVER and SWIPE mode. I kept the map extent, native resolution (1 pixel = 10 m), land-cover product, and rendering consistent between the two dates. I compared 2017 with 2024, then checked 2017 against 2025 to see whether the pattern persisted. The change panel reported Water +0.1%, Built Area −0.4%, and Rangeland +0.2% for 2017–2024. For 2017–2025, it reported Trees −0.6% and Rangeland +0.6%. The 2024–2025 comparison additionally reported Trees −0.7%, Built Area +0.4%, and Rangeland +0.3%.

The evidence

What I found

Between 2017 and 2024, the change panel reported a small increase in Water and Rangeland and a decrease in Built Area within the selected map extent. In the 2017–2025 comparison, Trees decreased and Rangeland increased. The 2024–2025 comparison showed a decrease in Trees and increases in both Built Area and Rangeland. The reported pattern therefore varied with the comparison period.

A separate change that the classification cannot directly show is the conversion of the commercial building at 265 Strand into NYU London's academic centre in 2024. The building may remain classified as Built Area despite its changed function. This is a change in building use, not necessarily a change in land cover.

What this does not show

I am using the Built Area class to stand for developed urban land, and it might fail to represent changes in building function because the classification does not distinguish commercial offices from university facilities. This is a limitation of the class definitions, not necessarily an error.

The mapped changes do not establish that NYU London's redevelopment caused changes in land-cover classes across the selected area. The comparison covers a wider area and does not identify the cause of each change.
