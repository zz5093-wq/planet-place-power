Land Cover, Then and Now

The question

How did land cover around central London change between 2017 and 2025, and what kinds of change cannot be captured by a land-cover map? My study site is NYU London's academic centre at 265 Strand. The building was converted from commercial use into a university facility, benefiting students and staff through new learning spaces. I wanted to examine how the pattern and distribution of land-cover classes relate to changes in the urban environment.

The data

I used the Sentinel-2 10 m Land Use/Land Cover Time Series produced by Esri and Impact Observatory. I selected 2017 and 2025 for the main comparison, and 2024 for an additional check because NYU London moved into its new academic centre that year. The dataset has a spatial resolution of 10 m. The layer is provided under the Esri Master License Agreement, while the source land-cover data is licensed under CC BY 4.0.

The method

I located 265 Strand in the Land Cover Explorer and selected LAND COVER and SWIPE mode. I kept the same map extent, native resolution (1 pixel = 10 m), land-cover categories, and rendering for both dates. The main comparison was 2017 versus 2025. I then changed the earlier year to 2024, keeping 2025 as the later year, to check whether the observed pattern remained consistent.

The change panel showed Trees decreasing by 0.6% and Rangeland increasing by 0.6% between 2017 and 2025. For 2024–2025, Trees decreased by 0.7%, Built Area increased by 0.4%, and Rangeland increased by 0.3%. These values refer to the selected map extent rather than the individual university building.

The evidence

Open the interactive land-cover comparison: 2017 vs 2025

What I found

The 2017–2025 comparison showed a small decrease in Trees and an increase in Rangeland across the mapped area. The 2024–2025 comparison showed a similar direction of change for these two classes, although Built Area also increased in the shorter comparison. This suggests that the reported pattern depends partly on the years selected.

However, another important change is not directly visible: the conversion of the commercial building at 265 Strand into NYU London's academic centre in 2024. The land-cover classification cannot distinguish a commercial office building from a university building, as both can fall under Built Area.

What this does not show

I am using the Built Area class to stand for developed urban land, and it might fail to represent changes in building function because the classification does not distinguish commercial offices from university facilities. This is a limitation of the class definitions rather than necessarily a classification error.

The comparison also cannot establish that the reported changes in Trees or Rangeland were caused by the redevelopment of 265 Strand.
